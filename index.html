Tracing alterations in a system with several generations of duct changes

A system changed in stages can hide how its sections connect. If your Niagara-on-the-Lake home has an addition, a moved dryer or ventilator, or ducts of mixed material, start by listing what you can see of each change. Next, note who made it if you know and any dates or papers you hold. Photographs of each place where materials change help. Last, mark what is unknown, because a provider can then quote against the real arrangement rather than an assumed one.

  1. Return grilles

    Openings that pull room air back into the system.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    Blower assembly, coil and the cabinet around them.

  4. Supply trunk

    The main duct leaving the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    The individual runs that split off toward each room.

  6. Supply registers

    Where air comes back into each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

  • Each change you know of

    Describe each addition, rerouting or equipment swap, who did it and roughly when, marking anything you are only guessing.

  • Where old meets new

    Photograph the places where duct material, size or finish changes, and note which registers sit on each side of the change.

  • Papers and unknowns

    Gather invoices, plans, manuals or inspection notes you hold, and write down plainly what nobody can confirm about the work.

Questions About Your Niagara-on-the-Lake Request

What sort of website is Niagara-on-the-Lake Duct Cleaning, and does anyone there clean ducts?

When you send a request, our enquiry team reviews it, and it may then go to a provider. Niagara-on-the-Lake Duct Cleaning is an enquiry website, not a contractor, and nobody who cleans ducts works for it. Sending a request books nothing and starts no work. Whichever provider receives it must identify themselves and confirm they cover your address before anything is arranged, and prices and timing are settled with that provider, not here.

Who handles a request sent from Niagara-on-the-Lake, and does it confirm a provider or visit?

You and a provider who has identified themselves agree anything that follows, in writing, before work begins. Sending shares your description and photographs with our enquiry team for review. It does not confirm a provider, coverage, a visit, a price or a time, and it does not start any work. Keep a copy of the request for yourself.

Who can approve duct work in a Niagara-on-the-Lake house with parts I did not change?

The person who owns or controls the system decides, which may be you, a co-owner, a landlord or a condominium corporation. If an earlier owner made changes, their papers may still help. Say in your request who can authorize work and who can give access, so the provider can ask the right person. Lease or sale questions belong with a real estate representative or lawyer.

Can one request cover the dryer vent and the ducts in my Niagara-on-the-Lake house?

Yes, one request can mention both, though they are separate systems and a provider may scope them separately. If the dryer vent was rerouted when the house was altered, describe that route apart from the furnace ducts, with its own photographs. Expect the written scope to list each system on its own lines. Whether one provider handles both is for them to confirm.

What do I write if I do not know who changed a section?

Write unknown, and describe only what you can see: the material, the shape, where it starts and where it ends. A provider can work from observations, and a gap in the history is better stated than guessed. Do not invent dates. Photographs of each end of the unfamiliar section often say more than a remembered story. Label guesses as guesses.

Does finding one altered section mean the rest of the ducts need work?

No. One altered section shows only that section; it does not describe the rest of the system, and a single dusty register is no better a guide. Ask the provider to separate what was observed in one place from what is assumed elsewhere, and to say in writing which findings apply to which sections. Treat any claim beyond that with caution.

What if I smell gas or suspect carbon monoxide near a changed vent?

Leave the building and contact the gas supplier or emergency responders; do not wait for a duct cleaning request. Questions about fuel, combustion, carbon monoxide or electrical safety need appropriately qualified diagnosis, and a cleaning inquiry is not the right route for them. Mention the change in your note only after you are safe. Do not try to open or move any equipment yourself.

Can a business tenant in Niagara-on-the-Lake send a request for altered ductwork?

Yes, a tenant can describe the space, the changes they know of and who manages the building. The provider will still need approval from whoever controls the system, usually the landlord, property manager or condominium corporation. Say in your request what you are allowed to authorize, so our enquiry team can note it for the provider. As a separate system, kitchen exhaust is raised separately.
